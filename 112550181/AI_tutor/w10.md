## ② My Misconception

### Before: I thought…

I did not have a major misconception that required revising an answer. However, I mainly viewed pruning and memoization as techniques for making a recursive search faster, without explicitly distinguishing practical speedups from worst-case complexity or identifying exactly what information should be cached.

### Now: I understand…

Pruning can avoid exploring branches that cannot lead to a feasible solution, but it does not necessarily change the exponential worst-case complexity of brute-force 0/1 knapsack search.

Memoization works by reusing results for identical subproblems. For knapsack, the relevant state is the remaining items and the remaining capacity—not the items previously selected or the value already accumulated. If two paths reach the same state, they can share the maximum **additional** value obtainable from that state, but they must add that value to their own accumulated totals.

I also understand that optimal substructure alone does not guarantee a speedup from memoization. There must be overlapping subproblems, and the benefit depends on how many distinct states are encountered.
