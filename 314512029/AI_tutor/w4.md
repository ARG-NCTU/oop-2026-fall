### Question 2

**Statement:** If a subclass defines its own `__init__`, Python automatically runs the parent class's `__init__` first.

**My initial answer:** True. I assumed construction always started by initializing the parent automatically.

**AI hint:** In the lecture's `Person` and `Student` examples, why do their constructors explicitly call `Animal.__init__` or `Person.__init__`?

**My revised answer:** False. Once a subclass supplies its own initializer, it must explicitly invoke the parent initializer when the inherited state needs to be established.

**AI feedback:** Correct. A subclass with no initializer may inherit the parent's initializer, but an overriding initializer replaces that lookup result.
